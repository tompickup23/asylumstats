**The fall in asylum support is a southern fall.** At 30 September 2026 there were 93,453 people on asylum support in the UK, 18,198 fewer than a year earlier. London, the East of England, the South East and the South West account for **12,660** of that fall, **70%**. A year earlier those four regions held 34.6% of everyone on support. They now hold 27.8%.

Three parts of the UK had more people on support than a year before: Scotland, the East Midlands and Wales.

**Hotels closed almost everywhere. Dispersal housing grew in only a few places.** The Home Office table splits support by type of accommodation. Contingency accommodation, which is mostly hotels, fell in every region and nation except Wales, where it moved from 99 to 102. Across the UK it fell by 24,722, from 38,924 to 14,202.

Dispersal accommodation, longer-term housing in the community, rose by 5,962 across the UK. Two places took most of that rise:

| | Contingency, change in the year | Dispersal, change in the year |
|---|---|---|
| London | -7,433 | +242 |
| Scotland | -985 | +2,254 |
| East Midlands | -1,222 | +1,697 |
| United Kingdom | -24,722 | +5,962 |

Scotland and the East Midlands together gained 3,951 dispersal places, 66% of the UK's increase. London lost 7,433 people from contingency accommodation and gained 242 in dispersal.

Per head, the north carries most. The North East has 25.6 people on asylum support for every 10,000 residents and the North West 25.0. The South West has 5.8 and the South East 6.7. The UK figure is 13.4.

The latest quarter shows the same regional pattern. Between 30 June and 30 September 2026 the UK total rose by 160. London fell by 1,018. Scotland rose by 774, the East Midlands by 523 and the North West by 463.

**What this does and does not show.** These are counts of people on support at a quarter end, by where they are housed. They do not follow individuals, so they cannot show that a person left a hotel in one region and was housed in another. They show where the system shrank and where it grew. Where people are placed is decided by the Home Office and its accommodation contractors, not by the people placed.
