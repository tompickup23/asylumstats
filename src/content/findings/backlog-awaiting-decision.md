**40,887 people** were waiting for the Home Office to make an initial decision on an asylum claim at 30 September 2026.

That is 719 more than three months earlier. The queue had fallen in each of the seven quarters before, and it last rose in the quarter to 30 September 2024.

| As at | Awaiting an initial decision |
|---|---|
| 30 June 2023 | 175,457 (the peak) |
| 31 December 2024 | 124,802 |
| 31 December 2025 | 64,426 |
| 31 March 2026 | 48,758 |
| 30 June 2026 | 40,168 |
| 30 September 2026 | 40,887 |

It stopped falling because claims now outrun decisions. In the quarter to September 2026 there were **18,000 new claims** against **17,558 initial decisions**, a gap of 442. In the quarter before, decisions led claims by 4,943.

The Home Office's own release says the number "increased slightly in the latest quarter" and describes a levelling off in the downward trend.

**What that does and does not tell you.** A shorter queue is not the same as a smaller system. 93,453 people were on asylum support at the same date, because a decision does not end support the day it lands, and refusals enter the appeal pipeline and do not leave the system. The queue is the part of the bill that responds to caseworking. The rest responds to arrivals, appeals and returns.

Of the initial decisions made in the quarter, 5,791 were grants of protection, 9,184 refusals, 2,157 withdrawals and 426 administrative outcomes.

**Updated 27 November 2026.** This page previously gave the position at 30 June 2026, 40,168, and said the queue was shrinking because decisions were outrunning claims. In the quarter to 30 September 2026 that reversed. The page also said the queue had fallen in six consecutive quarters to June 2026; the series shows seven.
