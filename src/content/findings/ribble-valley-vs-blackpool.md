**Nine miles. Two different countries, on paper.**

| Council | On asylum support | Per 10,000 | Rank of 361 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyndburn | 424 | 48.8 | 2nd |
| Burnley | 461 | 45.7 | 9th |
| Pendle | 451 | 45.0 | 10th |
| Preston | 409 | 24.9 | 46th |
| Blackpool | 237 | 16.5 | 89th |
| **Ribble Valley** | **32** | **4.8** | **212th** |

Figures as at 30 September 2026.

Ribble Valley borders Hyndburn, Burnley and Pendle. All four are Lancashire districts, all four fall under the same Serco prime accommodation contract for the North West, and the same company decides placement across all of them. Three are in the national top ten per head of population. The fourth is in the bottom half.

**Why the split is this sharp.** Dispersal accommodation is procured from the private rented sector, so it follows cheap housing rather than population. Ribble Valley has the most expensive housing in Lancashire and almost no dispersal stock. Hyndburn has the cheapest, and 424 people on support in a district of 87,000.

**What changed since this page was written.** It originally compared Ribble Valley with Blackpool, on the position at 31 December 2025, and put the gap at 8.5 times. Blackpool's supported population has since fallen from 577 to 237, so it is no longer the Lancashire outlier and the comparison has moved to the authorities that are: Hyndburn, Burnley and Pendle.

**Updated 27 November 2026.** At 30 June 2026 this page put Hyndburn at 13.7 times Ribble Valley's rate, when Ribble Valley had 24 people on support at 3.6 per 10,000. At 30 September 2026 it has 32, at 4.8 per 10,000, and Hyndburn has 424 at 48.8, so the gap is 10.2 times. It is still wider than the 8.5 times this page first reported.

The dispersal system is meant to share the load. Across one district boundary in East Lancashire it varies by a factor of ten.
