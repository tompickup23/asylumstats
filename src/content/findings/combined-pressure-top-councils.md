> **Correction, 12 October 2026.** Three of the index's five domains, police
> recorded crime, adult social care spend and SEND growth, came from 25-authority
> files typed into this site's source by hand in April 2026, with no raw file or
> script behind them. They could not be traced to ONS, NHS Digital or DfE tables.
> The files gave adult social care and SEND figures for district councils, which
> run neither service, and filed Preston, Staffordshire and Worcestershire under
> the wrong area codes. The scores and ranks below, including Blackburn with
> Darwen's **83.4**, Blackpool's **79.9** and Pendle's **78.9**, are therefore not
> supported by published data and should not be relied on. They have not been
> recomputed here, because the index needs rebuilding on the published tables
> first. On those tables Blackpool has the highest recorded crime rate (146.5 per
> 1,000, year ending March 2026) and the highest adult social care spend per adult
> (£884, 2024-25) of the 25 areas with a figure, but not the fastest SEND growth.

The composite index combines five domains at local authority level. Each is converted to a percentile rank against every area holding that dataset, then averaged across the domains an area has:

1. Asylum support rate per 10,000 residents, against all 361 UK local authorities
2. Demographic change, the absolute annual percentage-point move in White British share
3. Police recorded crime per 1,000
4. SEND demand growth, the five-year EHCP increase
5. Adult social care spend per head

Columns after the score are percentile ranks, where 100 is the most pressured.

**What changed, and why.** This page previously named Blackpool, Preston and Blackburn with Darwen, computed on the asylum position at 31 December 2025. Blackburn and Blackpool remain. Preston has fallen to seventh, and Pendle has taken the third place, entirely on the asylum domain: Preston's supported population rate has come down while Pendle's has not. Preston still ranks in the top three of the 25 tracked areas on crime, social care and SEND, and it has the lowest demographic-change percentile of the seven, at 12.

**Three caveats the earlier version did not carry.** The crime, social care and SEND datasets cover 25 local authorities, so an area outside those 25 scores on fewer domains and is not directly comparable. The domains are different vintages: crime is year ending March 2024, social care and SEND are 2023/24, the asylum figure is 30 June 2026 and the demographic rate derives from Census 2021. And the ranking will move whenever any of the five publishes, which is the point of dating it.

Until this revision the index compared each area against a list of 25 numbers typed into the source by hand. Those lists have been replaced by the live distributions, so the percentiles above are computed against the data rather than against a snapshot of it.

This index measures the co-occurrence of pressures. It does not claim that any one domain causes another.
