**£5.77 million per day on asylum hotels.** That is the number the Home Office publishes. It is real. It is sourced. And it is only 36% of the actual bill.

The true cost of the UK asylum system is approximately **£5.8 billion per year**. That is £170 for every income taxpayer in the country. Every year.

Nobody has published this figure before. The Home Office publishes hotel costs. The NAO publishes accommodation contracts. The MoJ publishes legal aid. Nobody adds them all up. So we did.

## The full breakdown

### Accommodation: £2.7 billion

The biggest single cost. Three private companies - Serco, Mears Group, and Clearsprings Ready Homes - run every asylum hotel, dispersal house, and reception centre in the country. The 10-year contract was originally forecast at £4.5 billion. It is now forecast at **£15.3 billion**.

- Hotels: £2.1 billion (30,657 people at an average £119/night)
- Dispersal accommodation: ~£500 million (66,683 people in flats and houses)
- Initial and contingency accommodation: ~£100 million

Hotels house 35% of the asylum population but consume 76% of the accommodation budget.

Source: NAO, "The Home Office's asylum accommodation contracts," May 2025.

### Home Office processing: £442 million

2,057 staff in the Asylum and Protection Directorate. Caseworkers, country specialists, policy teams, intake units. The Home Office made 160,077 initial decisions in 2025 but 100,625 new claims arrived. The queue shrinks slowly while the processing machine runs at full capacity.

Source: NAO Home Office Overview 2024-25.

### Local authority costs: £1.05 billion

Councils receive a £1,200 grant per asylum seeker per year. The actual cost of providing services - housing, social care, translation, community cohesion - is far higher.

Unaccompanied asylum-seeking children (UASC) cost £143 per child per night - £52,195 per year. 6,540 UASC are currently in local authority care. The Home Office grant for UASC was £636 million in 2024-25.

Failed asylum seekers with no recourse to public funds cost local authorities a further £55-65 million per year. Homelessness requests from people leaving asylum accommodation have risen 634% in two years.

Source: NAO December 2025; Home Office UASC Funding Instructions; NRPF Network.

### Border and Channel operations: £500 million

The UK pays France ~£159 million per year to patrol the Channel. Border Force runs interception and processing operations at Dover, Manston, and Western Jet Foil. Since 2017, the government has spent £3.77 billion on contracts to manage small boat crossings.

41,472 people crossed the Channel by small boat in 2025. 89% of all detected illegal entries.

Source: House of Commons Library CBP-9681; University of York/Liverpool research, April 2025.

### Healthcare (NHS): £236 million (estimated)

Asylum seekers are entitled to free GP registration, hospital treatment, mental health services, maternity care, and prescriptions. They are exempt from the Immigration Health Surcharge.

No government body publishes asylum-specific NHS costs. This estimate uses 60% of the NHS average per-capita cost (£3,462), adjusted for the younger age profile of the asylum population.

Source: Estimated from NHS Digital per-capita data and asylum population demographics.

### Education: £170 million (estimated)

Approximately 20,000 children of asylum-seeking families attend UK schools. Per-pupil funding averages £6,000 per year. English as an Additional Language (EAL) support adds £595-£1,595 per child for the first three years.

Neither the Home Office nor the DfE publishes the number of asylum seeker children in schools.

Source: Estimated from Home Office family group data and DfE National Funding Formula.

### Enforcement and detention: £310 million

Immigration Removal Centres cost £117 million per year to operate. An enforced removal costs £48,800 per person. A voluntary return costs £4,300.

The Rwanda scheme, before it was scrapped, cost £715 million and achieved zero involuntary deportations.

Source: NAO December 2025; Home Office return cost data; Detention Action.

### Subsistence payments: £220 million

107,003 people receive financial support. The rate is £49.18 per week in self-catered accommodation, £8.86-£9.95 per week in full-board (hotels).

Source: NAO December 2025.

### Tribunals and courts: £117 million

The Immigration and Asylum Chamber of the First-tier Tribunal costs £79.5 million per year to run. The backlog has reached 121,000 open cases. Mean clearance time: 60 weeks for an asylum appeal.

The Home Office spends a further £22 million per year on its own appeal representation.

Source: HMCTS Tribunal Statistics; NAO December 2025.

### Legal aid: £60 million

Immigration is the largest non-family category of civil legal aid. The Legal Aid Agency spent approximately £60 million on immigration and asylum cases in 2024-25. The government announced a 30% fee increase for immigration legal aid, the first in nearly 30 years.

Source: Legal Aid Agency quarterly statistics.

## What this means for you

- **£170 per income taxpayer per year** (34.1 million taxpayers, HMRC 2024-25)
- **£209 per household per year** (27.8 million households, ONS)
- **£15.9 million per day** nationally
- **£148 per asylum seeker per day** (all costs, not just hotels)

The hotel bill (£62/taxpayer) is only 36% of the true figure.

## What this does NOT include

This calculation excludes:
- Post-decision costs for people granted refugee status (benefits, housing, employment support)
- Criminal justice costs from trafficking and exploitation cases
- Economic opportunity cost of banning asylum seekers from working
- Long-term fiscal impact (Dutch research estimates a lifetime net cost of EUR 400,000 per asylum seeker)
- The £715 million spent on the Rwanda scheme (sunk cost, no ongoing liability)

## The aid budget trick

£2.8 billion of the asylum bill is classified as Official Development Assistance. This means the UK's effective foreign aid to developing countries is £2.8 billion less than headline figures suggest. The asylum system consumes 20% of the entire UK aid budget.
