**£2.1 billion on asylum hotel accommodation in 2024/25**, which works out at **£5.77 million a day**. That sits inside £4 billion spent on asylum support altogether.

**This is a 2024/25 figure, and it is the newest hotel-specific one anybody publishes.** It is on this page dated rather than presented as today's rate, because the thing it paid for has changed underneath it.

**The hotel population has roughly halved since.** The Home Office's own quarterly series:

| As at | In hotel accommodation | Share of all supported |
|---|---|---|
| 30 September 2023 | 56,018 | the peak |
| 31 December 2024 | 38,054 | 33.9% |
| 31 December 2025 | 30,657 | 28.7% |
| 31 March 2026 | 20,885 | 21.4% |
| 30 June 2026 | 16,021 | 17.2% |

**What the newer accounts do say.** The Home Office Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26 puts asylum support, resettlement and accommodation at £4,181 million for the year to March 2026, down from £4,513 million. Across 365 days that is £11.5 million a day, but it is a different base: it covers all accommodation and support, not hotels, so it cannot be compared with the £5.77 million above. Two numbers that count different things are not a trend.

**On the NAO.** The National Audit Office has not published a per-day figure. Its reporting covers the accommodation contracts, where hotels were around 76% of the cost in the first seven months of 2024-25, £1.3 billion of an estimated £1.7 billion. That is a seven-month split and reads about 40% high if it is treated as a full year.

The ten-year asylum accommodation contract is forecast at £15.3 billion. 40,168 people were waiting for an initial decision at 30 June 2026, down from 64,426 at the end of 2025.
