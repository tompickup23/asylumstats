**92 local authorities** are projected to have a White British population below 50% by 2051 under the central scenario.

This is not a linear extrapolation. It is a **cohort-component demographic model** — the same methodology used by the ONS for national population projections and by the University of Leeds NEWETHPOP project for ethnic projections. The model accounts for:

- **Age structure**: younger minority populations grow faster through higher birth rates in fertile age bands
- **Ethnic fertility differentials**: Pakistani/Bangladeshi TFR ~2.4 vs White British ~1.6, with modelled convergence over time
- **Mortality differentials**: ethnic-specific survival rates from ONS linked mortality studies
- **Migration**: internal migration (Census 2021 patterns) + international migration (3 ONS scenarios)
- **Fertility convergence**: the central scenario assumes ethnic fertility rates converge halfway toward the national average by 2061

**Nine scenarios** were modelled (3 fertility × 3 migration). The range: from 52 areas (low diversity scenario) to 134 areas (high diversity scenario) projected below 50% WBI by 2051.

The model was validated by backcasting NEWETHPOP's 2011-based predictions against Census 2021 actuals. NEWETHPOP systematically underestimated diversity growth — over-predicting White British population share in 95% of areas (mean error: 3.95 percentage points).

**Data**: Census 2021 base population (ONS RM032 + TS007A, 318 local authorities). 12 ethnic groups, 18 five-year age bands, 2 sexes. Projection period: 2021–2071.
