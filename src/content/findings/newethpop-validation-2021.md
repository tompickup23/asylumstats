**The UK diversified faster than the best academic model predicted.**

NEWETHPOP (University of Leeds, Rees, Norman, Wohland, Lomax & Clark) is the most cited ethnic population projection for UK local authorities. Published in 2016, it projected ethnic composition from Census 2011 to 2061 using a cohort-component model with age-specific fertility, mortality, and migration rates by ethnic group.

Census 2021 gave us the answer. We compared NEWETHPOP's prediction for 2021 against what actually happened across 296 local authorities.

**Result: the model over-predicted White British population share in 282 out of 296 areas (95%).** Average error: 3.95 percentage points. Only 16% of areas were accurate to within 1 percentage point.

The worst misses were in London and the Thames Gateway — areas where international migration accelerated beyond the model's assumptions. Thurrock was off by 17.2pp (predicted 83% WB, actual 66%). Greenwich, Barking & Dagenham, and Havering were each off by 15+ points.

This matters because every ethnic projection currently in use — including those cited by policymakers — inherits this same systematic bias. If the academic gold standard underestimated diversity growth by an average of 4 percentage points over just 10 years, then forward projections to 2050 or 2060 are likely understating the pace of demographic change significantly.

**Data:** NEWETHPOP Leeds2 (ONS-aligned scenario) vs ONS Census 2021 TS021. Analysis covers all English and Welsh local authorities where both datasets overlap. Validation methodology and full error table available on request.
